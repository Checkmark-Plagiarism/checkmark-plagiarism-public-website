## 1. The Accessibility and Equity Crisis: Assistive Technology Under Algorithmic Attack

In the contemporary K-12 and post-secondary educational landscape, writing instruction and authentic assessment have been profoundly disrupted by the emergence of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI). In response, hundreds of school districts, colleges, and testing consortia hastily adopted first-generation commercial AI detectors and static similarity checkers. These legacy tools analyze submitted text through crude statistical metrics—chiefly **perplexity** (the statistical measure of word predictability) and **burstiness** (the variation in sentence length and structural rhythm).

While marketed as automated integrity safeguards, these probabilistic algorithms have triggered an acute civil rights crisis across special education departments nationwide. Students with documented disabilities—including **dysgraphia, dyspraxia, Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), traumatic brain injury (TBI), and fine-motor orthopedic impairments**—rely on legally mandated Assistive Technologies (AT) to access the general education curriculum. Crucially, the operational mechanics of these assistive tools directly mimic the statistical signatures that commercial AI checkers flag as synthetic.

### Why Assistive Writing Tools Trigger False-Positive AI Flags

Special Education Case Managers and Section 504 Coordinators routinely encounter four primary technical triggers that cause legacy detection software to wrongly flag accommodated students:

#### 1. The Speech-to-Text (STT) "Acoustic Buffer Injection" Trap
When a student with severe dysgraphia or motor apraxia speaks into voice-recognition software (such as Apple Dictation, Google Voice Typing, or Dragon NaturallySpeaking), the operating system's speech engine processes the acoustic stream in local memory before rendering the transcribed text into the word processor in rapid 30-to-80-word blocks. 
* **The Legacy Detection Flaw:** Standard document version checkers (such as default Google Docs or Microsoft Word version history) do not log the microsecond-level phoneme parsing or vocal pauses of the speaker. Instead, they record an instantaneous insertion of a complete block of text. To a basic diff algorithm or coarse revision inspector, this acoustic buffer insertion appears completely identical to a student copying and pasting a generated paragraph directly from ChatGPT or Claude.

#### 2. Lexical Compression and Constrained Perplexity in Word Prediction Engines
Assistive word-prediction software (e.g., Co:Writer, Read&Write by Texthelp, WordQ) supports students with specific learning disabilities by predicting upcoming vocabulary based on phonemic cues, grammatical rules, and contextual syntax. 
* **The Legacy Detection Flaw:** By design, word-prediction algorithms suggest grammatically standard, highly probable lexical choices to help students overcome vocabulary retrieval barriers. When an autistic student or a student with executive dysfunction composes an essay utilizing word prediction, the resulting prose exhibits uniformly low perplexity (high mathematical predictability). Commercial AI detectors interpret this high statistical consistency as machine-generated text, effectively penalizing the student for utilizing the exact software required by their IEP.

#### 3. Auditory Review Loops and Atypical Pause-Burst Dynamics
Students who utilize Text-to-Speech (TTS) screen readers (such as Kurzweil 3000 or Immersive Reader) engage in an auditory revision loop: they draft a sentence, trigger TTS playback to listen to the syntax through headphones, pause for 45 to 90 seconds of cognitive reflection, and then execute rapid targeted edits.
* **The Legacy Detection Flaw:** Probabilistic classifiers and generic proctoring algorithms misinterpret these extended cognitive pauses as "student disengagement" or "unauthorized external research," while treating the subsequent rapid revision bursts as pre-generated text insertions.

#### 4. Non-Linear Drafting and Modular "Bricolage" Assembly
Students with ADHD and executive functioning deficits rarely write in a traditional linear trajectory (Introduction $ightarrow$ Body Paragraphs $ightarrow$ Conclusion). Instead, they frequently compose in fragmented, non-linear modules: drafting thematic bullet points in external approved scratchpads (e.g., Apple Notes, Google Keep, Notion), writing the evidentiary conclusion first, and subsequently copying their own synthesized modules into the learning management system (LMS) editor.
* **The Legacy Detection Flaw:** Static plagiarism checkers and naive version tools flag these legitimate structural imports as suspicious external clipboard drops, lacking the contextual history to verify that the student authored every word.

* **Writing Task Avoidance and Mutism:** The student develops acute writing phobia, refusing to complete written assignments or engage in classroom activities out of fear that their genuine effort will be condemned.
* **Harmful Accommodation Masking:** To avoid algorithmic suspicion, students deliberately disable their speech-to-text tools, word prediction, and auditory screen readers. Attempting to write manually or type without assistive scaffolds leads to severe physical fatigue, hand cramping, cognitive burnout, and plummeting academic achievement.
* **Destruction of the Multi-Tiered Support Alliance:** The foundational trust between the student, the special education case manager, the general education teacher, and parents is fractured by an adversarial disciplinary process based on unscientific software.

### 1. Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA, 20 U.S.C. § 1400 et seq.)

Under IDEA, public school districts must provide every eligible student with a disability a **Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE)** in the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE) through an Individualized Education Program (IEP).

* **The Assistive Technology Mandate (34 CFR § 300.105):** The IEP team is legally required to evaluate and provide necessary Assistive Technology devices and services. When an IEP includes accommodations such as *"Speech-to-text software for all written responses exceeding two sentences"* or *"Word prediction software for essay composition,"* these stipulations are **federally binding legal requirements**.
* **Denial of FAPE Through Algorithmic Discipline:** If a general education instructor issues a failing grade, withholds academic credit, or initiates a disciplinary referral because an uncalibrated commercial AI detector flagged the student's voice-dictated essay, the district has committed a substantive denial of FAPE.
* **Manifestation Determination Review (MDR) Requirements (34 CFR § 300.530(e)):** IDEA strictly prohibits disciplinary sanctions resulting in a disciplinary change of placement without an MDR meeting. Charging an accommodated student with academic misconduct based on the linguistic characteristics of their assistive technology violates federal procedural safeguards.

### 2. Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 U.S.C. § 794)

Section 504 prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities in any program receiving federal financial assistance.

* **Mandatory Accommodation Implementation:** Section 504 Accommodation Plans frequently grant accommodations such as **1.5x or 2.0x extended writing time**, **frequent sensory/motor breaks**, and **use of a word processor with spell-check and speech dictation**. 
* **The Evidentiary Burden of Accommodation Fidelity:** When a student with a 504 plan performs poorly or is accused of taking unauthorized breaks during a timed digital exam, the legal burden falls on the district to prove whether the mandated extended time and break intervals were faithfully provided and utilized. Without granular writing telemetry, districts cannot objectively demonstrate compliance during administrative audits.

### 3. Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA, 42 U.S.C. § 12131)

Title II extends non-discrimination mandates to all public entities, including local educational agencies (LEAs), state testing agencies, and public universities.

* **Prohibition of Algorithmic Disparate Impact:** Under 28 CFR § 35.130(b)(3), a public entity may not utilize *"criteria or methods of administration"* that subject individuals with disabilities to discrimination or have the purpose or effect of defeating or substantially impairing accomplishment of educational objectives. Deploying an AI detector that systematically flags neurodivergent writing styles or assistive software inputs constitutes unlawful algorithmic disparate impact.

### 4. Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Enforcement and Audit Exposure

The U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights (OCR) actively investigates complaints alleging discrimination based on disability. When parents file OCR complaints regarding wrongful academic integrity sanctions levied against accommodated students, districts face exhaustive federal compliance audits. Potential consequences include:
1. **Mandated Expungement:** Federal orders to expunge all disciplinary records, misconduct flags, and academic penalties from the student's cumulative file.
2. **Compensatory Education:** District-funded compensatory tutoring, counseling, and writing instruction to remediate educational harm.
3. **Federal Funding Scrutiny:** Risk to Title I and IDEA federal grant allocations for persistent failure to enforce non-discrimination policies.

### 1. Deconstructing the Telemetry Event Stream

Every digital writing session comprises thousands of micro-events that reveal the writer's authentic cognitive workflow:

1. **Inter-Keystroke Interval (IKI) and Dwell-Time:** The time elapsed between consecutive keystrokes (IKI) and the duration a single key remains depressed (dwell-time). Human drafting is characterized by rich, fluctuating IKI distributions (e.g., 80ms to 450ms during active typing, interspersed with 2-to-15-second cognitive planning pauses). Conversely, mechanical script injection or automated pasting exhibits flat, near-zero IKI profiles.
2. **Acoustic Speech-to-Text Signature Profiling:** When a student dictates through an operating system's speech engine, the telemetry engine records a distinct profile: a 4-to-12-second acoustic capture interval with zero keyboard events, followed by an atomic text insertion event containing spoken punctuation tokens (`"new paragraph"`, `"comma"`, `"period"`), immediately followed by a 3-to-8-second auditory verification pause. This distinct rhythm differentiates speech-to-text from an illicit LLM paste.
3. **Word-Prediction Selection Telemetry:** When using Co:Writer or Read&Write, the telemetry logs 1-to-3 human keystrokes followed by an instant word insertion triggered by a `Tab`, `Enter`, or number-key event, occurring at a natural human reaction velocity (400ms–800ms). This captures genuine human decision-making.
4. **Active Composition vs. Cognitive & Sensory Rest Pauses:** The telemetry engine categorizes session time into three distinct operational states:
   $$	ext{Total Session Duration} = T_{	ext{active}} + T_{	ext{cognitive pause}} + T_{	ext{sensory break}}$$
   * **Active Drafting Time ($T_{	ext{active}}$):** Continuous keystrokes, text editing, cursor navigation, and voice dictation.
   * **Cognitive Processing Pauses ($T_{	ext{cognitive pause}}$):** 15-to-180-second intervals where the student pauses to read prompt material, synthesize sources, listen to screen reader feedback, or plan structural phrasing while remaining in the active document window.
   * **Sensory/Motor Rest Breaks ($T_{	ext{sensory break}}$):** Extended pauses (5 to 20 minutes) authorized by 504/IEP plans for physical decompression, motor relaxation, or ADHD self-regulation, during which no document interactions occur.

By logging these distinct states, Checkmark provides 504 Coordinators with indisputable mathematical documentation verifying that a student utilizing 1.5x extended time genuinely utilized the accommodation for required cognitive processing and authorized rest breaks—not for off-task evasion or unauthorized collaboration.
