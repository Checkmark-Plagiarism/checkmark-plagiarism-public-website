### 1.1 The Operational Breakdown of Teacher-by-Teacher Configuration

When assignment auditing relies on individual teacher initiative, three systemic failures occur across secondary schools:

1. **Massive Coverage Blind Spots:** In a district with 500 secondary teachers, fewer than 40% consistently configure external tool settings correctly. Crucial writing milestones—such as 9th-grade diagnostic essays, 10th-grade social studies Document-Based Questions (DBQs), 11th-grade AP Capstone synthesis papers, and 12th-grade senior portfolios—slip through unaudited. When an academic integrity issue is later discovered during senior year or college application season, administrators find zero historical writing baselines.
2. **Campus-to-Campus Equity Gaps:** Students at High School A may be subjected to aggressive, uncalibrated AI detectors that penalize non-native English writers, while students at High School B receive no integrity feedback whatsoever. This creates severe legal and administrative vulnerabilities during grade appeals, honor board reviews, and district-wide GPA evaluations.
3. **Severe Instructional Friction:** Secondary teachers already dedicate 15–20 hours weekly to lesson planning and grading. Requiring them to navigate complex external tool menus, manage separate login credentials, or manually export/import student rosters causes widespread adoption fatigue and passive resistance.

### 1.2 The Threat of "Shadow IT" and Third-Party Browser Extensions

Faced with rising generative AI usage and lacking a seamless district solution, many well-intentioned teachers turn to **Shadow IT**: installing unauthorized browser extensions or copying and pasting student writing into free consumer AI detectors. 

This practice introduces existential liabilities for school districts:
* **FERPA & State Privacy Violations:** Uploading student prose containing personally identifiable information (PII), localized context, or disciplinary disclosures to unauthorized commercial platforms violates FERPA (34 CFR Part 99), COPPA (15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506), and state statutes (such as NY Ed Law § 2-d, Illinois SOPPA, and California SOPIPA).
* **Non-Consensual AI Model Training:** Free web detectors frequently monetize user data by harvesting submitted student essays into massive training lakes to pre-train and fine-tune commercial Large Language Models (LLMs).
* **Unreliable, Black-Box False Positives:** Consumer detectors generate single, opaque percentage scores (e.g., *"92% AI Generated"*) based on brittle linguistic heuristics. When teachers act on these unverified scores, innocent students are falsely accused, triggering contentious parent confrontations, formal grievances, and Office for Civil Rights (OCR) inquiries.

### 1.3 The Pedagogical Paradigm Shift: From Punitive Policing to Transparent Writing Telemetry

To resolve this crisis, district instructional technology leadership must lead a fundamental philosophical shift: moving from punitive policing to transparent, growth-oriented writing instruction. 

Checkmark Plagiarism's core guiding philosophy is **"Stop guessing, start trusting."** Academic integrity tools should never function as arbitrary, punitive black-box judges. Instead, edtech infrastructure must provide transparent, defensible, and objective "receipts"—combining passage-level linguistic distributions, side-by-side plagiarism source proof, patent-pending keystroke dynamics (Essay Playback™), and teacher-controlled rubric evaluations. 

By centralizing this capability through Canvas LTI 1.3 Advantage, districts protect student rights, empower educators with actionable coaching data, and eliminate the risks of unauthorized shadow IT.

### 2.1 Canvas Account Hierarchy: Root Account vs. Sub-Account Deployment

Canvas LMS organizes institutional data in a hierarchical structure:
1. **Root Account (District Level):** The master administrative tier governing global authentication, system integrations, developer keys, SIS roster integrations, and district-wide policies.
2. **Sub-Accounts (School/Campus Level):** Individual high schools, middle schools, or specialized centers (e.g., North High School, South High School, Virtual High School, CTE Academy).
3. **Sub-Account Categories/Departments:** Departmental nodes (e.g., English Language Arts, Social Sciences, Science, Career Technical Education).
4. **Courses & Sections:** The active instructional spaces where assignments are created, submissions occur, and grades are entered.

**The District Architectural Principle:**
Configuring Checkmark at the **Root Account level** ensures that the tool is automatically inherited by all existing and future sub-accounts. District administrators manage developer keys, privacy controls, and audit rules centrally, while school-level instructional leaders and classroom teachers benefit from zero-configuration automated auditing across 100% of courses.

### 2.2 Modern LTI Advantage 1.3 Security Framework

Unlike obsolete LTI 1.1 integrations that relied on static, symmetric shared secrets (OAuth 1.0a) vulnerable to packet interception and credential exposure, **LTI 1.3 Advantage** is built on modern, enterprise-grade identity and security standards:

* **OAuth 2.0 & OpenID Connect (OIDC):** Uses stateful, multi-step authorization handshakes. The LMS and Checkmark authenticate via signed JSON Web Tokens (JWTs), ensuring that student and educator identities are cryptographically validated without exposing raw credentials.
* **Asymmetric RSA-256 Public/Private Key Cryptography:** Canvas and Checkmark exchange public keys via JSON Web Key Sets (`keyset_url` / JWKS). Every launch request, submission webhook, and grade passback payload is signed with a private key and verified using the recipient's public key (JWS), making man-in-the-middle attacks and session spoofing mathematically impossible.
* **Replay Protection via Nonce & State Parameters:** Every OIDC launch generates a cryptographically unique `nonce` and `state` parameter that expires immediately after consumption, preventing session replay attacks.

### 3.1 Student FERPA/COPPA Compliance and Zero-Model-Training Guarantees

Under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), student essays, research papers, and writing telemetry constitute **protected education records**. School districts may only share these records with third-party vendors under the **"School Official"** exception (34 CFR § 99.31(a)(1)(i)), which mandates that the vendor:
1. Performs an institutional service for which the district would otherwise use employees;
2. Remains under the **direct control** of the district regarding the use and maintenance of education records;
3. Adheres to strict **redisclosure and purpose limitations**—meaning the vendor cannot repurpose student writing for commercial benefit, machine learning model training, or data broker monetization.

**Checkmark’s Ironclad Privacy Architecture:**
* **Zero Model Training on Student Submissions:** Checkmark never uses student prose, revision histories, or teacher grading comments to pre-train, fine-tune, or evaluate foundation AI models.
* **Ephemeral In-Memory Processing:** Raw student submissions are ingested into stateless, isolated memory buffers (RAM), analyzed for linguistic features and source matches, and immediately purged via cryptographic zeroization. No raw student essays are permanently cached on public cloud storage disks.
* **Salted District-Isolated Peer Hashing:** To detect student-to-student copying across high schools within the same district, Checkmark converts submitted text into non-invertible, cryptographically salted mathematical hash fingerprints (MinHash / Locality-Sensitive Hashing). The system can identify cross-school copying without ever storing the underlying raw prose, ensuring full compliance with state privacy statutes.

### 3.2 Eradicating Shadow IT: Legal Exposure of Chrome Extensions

When teachers independently install free AI detection Chrome extensions or web tools, they inadvertently breach district compliance policies:
* **Unvetted Data Sub-Processors:** Free browser extensions routinely transmit DOM content, browser cookies, and student essay text to unvetted overseas servers.
* **Lack of Data Processing Agreements (DPAs):** Standalone consumer tools operate under click-through Terms of Service (ToS) that explicitly state the vendor may retain, modify, and commercially exploit submitted content.
* **District Liability in Breaches:** If a free browser extension suffers a data breach exposing student essays and student PII, the school district remains legally liable for unauthorized data disclosure under state laws such as New York Education Law § 2-d and Illinois SOPPA.

Deploying Checkmark via Canvas Root LTI 1.3 completely eliminates the incentive for Shadow IT by providing faculty with a superior, native, district-approved solution embedded directly in SpeedGrader.

### 3.3 District-Wide Equity and Defensible Evidentiary Standards

Generic AI detectors that produce a single whole-document percentage score (e.g., *"85% AI"*) create severe equity issues across high schools:
* **False Positive Disparities:** Statistical perplexity-only detectors disproportionately flag English Language Learners (ELL), students with ASD (Autism Spectrum Disorder), and emerging writers whose structured syntax mimics AI patterns.
* **Indefensible Disciplinary Actions:** A whole-document percentage score is legally and pedagogically indefensible during parent appeals or formal hearings because it cannot explain *which* sentences were generated or *why*.

Checkmark establishes a standardized, district-wide evidentiary standard rooted in **multi-dimensional proof**:
1. **Passage-Level AI Highlighting:** Specific sentences are flagged with individual evidence cards and calibrated confidence sliders (Human Style vs. Typical AI Pattern), rather than condemning an entire essay.
2. **Honest Short-Text Guardrails:** Submissions under ~150 words display an honest `N/A` disclaimer, preventing false-positive flags on brief discussion posts and short-answer exit tickets.
3. **Patent-Pending Essay Playback™:** Keystroke dynamics reconstruct the authentic human writing process, providing unassailable visual proof that protects honest students from false accusations.

### 2. How does Checkmark ensure student essays are not used to train generative AI models?
Checkmark operates under a legally binding, enterprise **Zero Model Training Guarantee**. Student essays, revision histories, and grading feedback are never ingested into training datasets, fine-tuning pipelines, or algorithmic feature stores. ### 4. How does Checkmark handle peer plagiarism detection across different high schools without storing student essays in a public database?
Checkmark uses **Salted Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH)**. When another student submits a paper at a different high school within the district, the system compares the hash signatures. It detects verbatim or structural copying across district schools without ever storing or exposing raw student text in persistent databases.

### 5. Why is Essay Playback™ more reliable than third-party AI detection percentages?
Statistical AI detectors rely on linguistic predictability (perplexity and burstiness), which can yield false positives—especially on highly structured essays or non-native English writing. **Essay Playback™** bypasses statistical guesswork by providing **objective, empirical writing process telemetry**. Watching a student’s keystrokes, composing pauses, revision cycles, and external clipboard pastes provides conclusive, defensible proof of authentic human authorship.

### 6. Can our district customize rubrics and enable AI autograding for specific high schools or departments?
**Yes.** Through Canvas LTI 1.3 Deep Linking and the Checkmark Rubric Manager, curriculum coordinators can create and distribute standardized district writing rubrics (e.g., 9th-Grade Informational, AP History DBQ, CTE Technical Report) to specific sub-accounts, courses, or grade levels. ### 7. What happens if a student writes an essay in Google Docs or Microsoft 365 before submitting to Canvas?
Checkmark integrates natively with the Google Docs and Microsoft 365 ecosystems via Checkmark’s ecosystem telemetry. When students submit a Google Doc or Word document through Canvas, Checkmark ingests the rich revision history and reconstructs the complete **Essay Playback™** timeline inside SpeedGrader.
