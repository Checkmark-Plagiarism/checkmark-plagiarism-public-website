### 1. Ayres Sensory Integration (ASI) Fidelity Measure

In research and clinical practice, sensory integration therapy is defined by the **Ayres Sensory Integration (ASI) Fidelity Measure** (Parham et al., 2011). Authentic SI documentation must reflect the core tenets of ASI fidelity:
* **Physical Safety and Sensory Environment:** Adequate space, specialized impact-absorbing matting, and multiple ceiling suspension points rated for dynamic movement.
* **Rich Sensory Opportunities:** Intentional provision of vestibular (rotary, linear, vertical), proprioceptive (active resistance, joint compression, heavy work), and tactile (textures, deep pressure) inputs.
* **The "Just-Right" Challenge:** Designing activities that challenge the child's sensory-motor system without overwhelming their coping mechanisms or inducing behavioral breakdown.
* **Active Child Collaboration:** The therapist does not impose a passive schedule of exercises; the child actively participates in selecting, modifying, and navigating equipment to elicit an adaptive response.
* **Context of Play:** Interventions are framed within playful, engaging, self-directed activities that foster intrinsic motivation.

### 2. Standardized Sensory Assessment Architectures

Graduate OT students are trained to administer and interpret two primary standardized sensory assessment batteries:

#### A. Sensory Processing Measure, Second Edition (SPM-2)
The SPM-2 assesses sensory processing across multiple ecological environments (Home, Main Classroom, School Environments). Scores are reported as **T-scores** ($	ext{Mean} = 50$, $	ext{Standard Deviation} = 10$):
* **Typical Range:** T-score $40–59$
* **Some Problems:** T-score $60–69$
* **Definite Dysfunction:** T-score $70–80$

The assessment evaluates discrete sensory and motor domains:
1. **Vision (VIS)**
2. **Hearing (HEA)**
3. **Touch (TOU)**
4. **Taste and Smell (TAS)**
5. **Body Position (BOD / Proprioception)**
6. **Balance and Motion (BAL / Vestibular)**
7. **Planning and Ideas (PLA / Praxis)**
8. **Social Participation (SOC)**

#### B. Sensory Profile-2 (Winnie Dunn)
Based on Winnie Dunn’s Model of Sensory Processing, this tool evaluates the interaction between a child’s **Neurological Threshold** (High vs. Low) and **Behavioral Response / Self-Regulation Strategy** (Passive vs. Active):

### 4. SMART Goal Writing & Individualized Sensory Diet Engineering

In occupational therapy documentation, sensory processing findings must never stand alone as isolated neurobiological phenomena. Under insurance billing standards (Medicaid, commercial payers) and school district IEP (Individualized Education Program) frameworks, goals must be **SMART (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-bound)** and explicitly tied to **Occupational Performance Areas**:

```
$$	ext{Authentic OT Goal Formula: } [	ext{Client}] + [	ext{Occupational Task/ADL}] + [	ext{Level of Independence/Assistance}] + [	ext{Sensory-Motor Condition/Adaptation}] + [	ext{Measurement Metric}] + [	ext{Timeline}]$$
```

* **Exemplary Authentic Goal:** *"In 6 months, client will independently maintain seated postural stability on an air-cushioned disc at his school desk to complete a 10-minute written journal entry without falling from his chair or leaning on the desktop in 4 out of 5 consecutive school days."*
* **Flawed / Fabricated AI Goal:** *"In 6 months, client will improve his vestibular processing to 85% normal during swing therapy."* (Unmeasurable, clinically meaningless, detached from functional occupation).

#### Sensory Diet Architectures
An individualized sensory diet is a carefully scheduled, customized plan of sensory activities designed to help a child maintain an optimal state of arousal throughout the day. It incorporates:
1. **Calming / Organizing Inputs:** Deep touch pressure, slow rhythmic linear vestibular movement, active heavy work / joint compression (proprioception).
2. **Alerting Inputs:** Fast, irregular, rotary vestibular input, light touch, bright unpredictable lighting, sour/crunchy tastes.
3. **Proprioceptive "Reset" Mechanisms:** Heavy work activities (e.g., carrying weighted bins, wall push-ups, animal walks) that serve as the universal central nervous system modulator, dampening vestibular hypersensitivity while awakening lethargic systems.

### Anatomical & Clinical Hallucinations Generated by LLMs

When students feed case prompts into AI models, the resulting writeups display characteristic clinical contradictions that reveal synthetic authorship:

#### 1. The Psychometric Scoring Fantasy
Large Language Models frequently invent impossible score metrics. For instance, an LLM writeup might claim:
> *"On the SPM-2 Home Form, the client demonstrated a T-score of 42 (indicating Definite Dysfunction) and an 88% deficiency in the Balance and Motion subscale."*

*Clinical Reality:* In the SPM-2, a T-score of 42 is squarely within the **Typical Range** ($40–59$), while scores $\ge 70$ indicate Definite Dysfunction. Furthermore, the SPM-2 does not utilize "percentage deficiency" metrics; combining percentile rankings with inverted T-scores is a dead giveaway of statistical hallucination.

#### 2. The Gravitational Insecurity vs. Rotary Suspension Paradox
An AI-generated treatment plan might state:
> *"To address the client's severe gravitational insecurity and tactile defensiveness, the OT will place the child in a suspended bolster swing and initiate 15 minutes of multi-directional rotary swinging at high speeds to normalize vestibular pathways."*

*Clinical Reality:* This intervention is **strictly contraindicated**. Gravitational insecurity is an over-responsiveness to otolith stimulation characterized by intense emotional anxiety and autonomic fear when the center of gravity shifts or feet leave the floor. Subjecting a gravitationally insecure child to high-speed suspended rotary swinging can trigger profound autonomic panic, vagal syncope, vomiting, or extreme sensory trauma. Authentic clinical reasoning dictates starting with firm feet-on-the-ground linear movements and deep proprioceptive compression.

### Phase 1: Psychometric Concordance & Raw Data Verification
Before reading the student's narrative analysis, faculty cross-reference the submitted assessment report with the raw, scored evaluation protocols:
1. **Raw Score to Standard Score Verification:** Verify that raw item sums on the SPM-2 or Sensory Profile-2 correctly convert to published normative T-scores and percentile ranks.
2. **Qualitative-Quantitative Concordance:** Check whether the parent/teacher anecdotal comments recorded on the score sheets match the behavioral descriptions in the student’s clinical summary.
3. **Standard Deviation Boundaries:** Ensure that quadrant cutoffs (e.g., Dunn's Sensory Profile-2 $+2	ext{ SD}$ cutoff) are mathematically consistent with the reported standard scores.

### Phase 2: ASI Fidelity & Safety Spot-Checks
Evaluate the clinical viability and safety of the proposed sensory intervention plan:
1. **Contraindication Screening:** Does the plan prescribe rotary vestibular input to a child with seizure history, severe gravitational insecurity, or autonomic reactivity?
2. **Sensory Sequence Architecture:** Does every alerting or vestibular activity conclude with organizing proprioceptive input (heavy work, joint compression) to prevent post-treatment dysregulation?
3. **Occupational Linkage:** Are treatment activities tied to functional ADL, play, or school tasks, or are they isolated "sensory play" exercises lacking clinical rationale?

### Phase 3: Writing Process Telemetry Analysis with Essay Playback™
Using **Checkmark Essay Playback™**, faculty review the student's writing timeline at 1x to 8x speed to observe the authentic cognitive mechanics of clinical documentation:
* **The "Data-to-Narrative Synthesis Pause":** An authentic student clinician drafts incrementally. The playback reveals frequent pauses of 15–45 seconds where the student stops typing to reference the SPM-2 score sheet, calculate raw metrics, check normative tables, and formulate their narrative.
* **Natural Formulation & Revision Bursts:** Authentic student writing exhibits dynamic editing—re-typing awkward clinical phrases, refining diagnostic terminology (e.g., changing "clumsy" to "dyspraxic presentation"), and adjusting goal parameters.
* **The Mechanical AI Transcription Signature:** In contrast, a student who is copying an AI-generated draft from a secondary monitor or mobile phone displays steady, uninterrupted, monotonous keystrokes with zero reference pauses, zero structural revisions, and zero diagnostic problem-solving stalls.

### Phase 4: External Paste Inspection & Raw Clinical Log Verification
Checkmark's **External Paste Inspector** captures the exact text of every paste event, preserving original clipboard content even if the student subsequently edits the text:
* **Legitimate Clinical Pasting:** Pasting standardized clinic headers, standardized institutional SOAP templates, or excerpts from the child’s medical history chart.
* **Unauthorized AI Generation:** Pasting multi-paragraph, fully synthesized sensory diet narratives or comprehensive assessment summaries generated by external LLM tools in a single timestamped event.

### Phase 5: Supportive Restorative Clinical Reasoning Debrief
Rather than initiating an adversarial honor code prosecution, faculty use the objective playback evidence to conduct a supportive, educational debrief:
1. **Collaborative Timeline Review:** Sit down with the student and open the Checkmark Essay Playback™ together.
2. **Clinical Inquiry Protocol:** Ask the student open-ended questions: *"I noticed this 400-word sensory diet was pasted in at 11:42 PM. Walk me through the clinical reasoning behind selecting the suspended lycra hammock over the bolster swing for this specific child."*
3. **Diagnosing the Root Cause:** Distinguish between a student overwhelmed by clinical documentation time pressures vs. a student who fundamentally does not understand sensory integration theory.
4. **Remediation Pathways:** Provide targeted clinical coaching, require re-scoring of assessment protocols under faculty observation, and reinforce professional ethics under ACOTE standards.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 1. How do OT faculty differentiate between authorized AI grammar assistance and unauthorized clinical fabrication?
Authorized AI assistance includes using tools like Grammarly or spellcheck to correct typos, refine sentence flow, or clarify passive phrasing in clinical notes. In contrast, unauthorized clinical fabrication occurs when a student uses generative AI to invent pediatric behavioral observations, fabricate standardized test scores (e.g., SPM-2, Sensory Profile-2), synthesize clinical impressions, or generate complete sensory diets without having evaluated an actual client. Checkmark Essay Playback™ makes this distinction immediately clear: grammar corrections appear as brief, localized edits within student-drafted prose, whereas AI fabrication appears as large, uncomposed external paste events or monotonous, uninterrupted typing sequences.

### 2. Why do generic AI detectors trigger false positives on standardized OT clinical shorthand?
Generic AI detectors measure statistical perplexity (word choice unpredictability) and burstiness (sentence length diversity). In occupational therapy, clinical documentation relies heavily on standardized, formulaic phrasing and medical acronyms (`SI`, `ASI`, `WNL`, `ADLs`, `IADLs`, `SPM-2`, `ROM`, `WFL`, `PRN`, `FIM`, `COPM`, `AOTA`, `OTR/L`, `COTA`) to ensure precision and billing compliance. Because these professional abbreviations and standard phrasing are highly predictable, generic detectors mistakenly flag authentic student writing as machine-generated. Checkmark overcomes this flaw by combining passage-level linguistic analysis with keystroke telemetry, evaluating *how* the document was constructed rather than penalizing necessary medical terminology.

### 3. How does Essay Playback™ protect neurodivergent OT students who draft non-linearly?
Many neurodivergent graduate students (including individuals with ADHD, dyslexia, or autism) compose clinical notes in unconventional patterns—jumping between sections, brainstorming in fragmented bullet points, deleting large blocks of text, or experiencing extended pauses while processing complex diagnostic information. Traditional AI detectors often misinterpret these idiosyncratic writing patterns as anomalous or artificial. Checkmark Essay Playback™ captures the authentic, organic human drafting process keystroke-by-keystroke, providing undeniable visual proof of genuine creative effort and protecting neurodivergent students from unfounded accusations.

### 4. Can Checkmark detect students manually retyping AI-generated treatment plans from a phone or second monitor?
Yes. When a student composes an authentic clinical evaluation, their typing velocity is dynamic: fast bursts during familiar terminology, interspersed with frequent pauses (15–45 seconds) to consult raw score sheets, review reference norms, and formulate clinical hypotheses. When a student mechanically transcribes text from an external device (such as an iPhone or secondary laptop), the typing telemetry exhibits an unnatural, steady, unbroken cadence without cognitive synthesis pauses, and with near-zero conceptual revisions or structural deletions. Checkmark’s typing velocity analytics easily expose this transcription pattern.

### 5. How does Checkmark maintain HIPAA and FERPA compliance when evaluating student clinical case writeups?
Checkmark is built on an enterprise zero-retention security architecture. Student submissions, clinical evaluations, and fieldwork reflection journals are encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (AES-256). Crucially, student text is **never cached, shared, sold, or used to train public Large Language Models**. Furthermore, Checkmark integrates directly with university-managed Single Sign-On (Google SSO, Microsoft SSO) and LMS platforms (Canvas, Buzz, Google Classroom), ensuring that Protected Health Information (PHI) and student educational records remain strictly within institutional control.

Level II Fieldwork preceptors (OTR/L) are busy clinicians balancing heavy patient caseloads with student supervision. They often lack the time to investigate suspected AI usage in student clinical logs and SOAP notes. AFWCs can use Checkmark to centrally review student documentation submitted through Canvas or Buzz LMS. By reviewing Essay Playback™ timelines and paste reports before site visits or midterm FWPE evaluations, AFWCs can quickly identify students who are struggling with independent clinical reasoning and implement targeted academic remediation before clinical competency is compromised.

### 7. What restorative remediation steps should faculty take when an OT student submits an AI-generated sensory integration plan?
Rather than immediately referring the student to a punitive disciplinary committee, faculty should implement a restorative clinical learning protocol:
1. **Review the Playback Together:** Open the Checkmark Essay Playback™ with the student to establish an objective, transparent factual baseline.
2. **Explore Cognitive Roadblocks:** Discuss what led to the incident (e.g., anxiety over clinical performance, heavy caseload, confusion regarding sensory processing constructs).
3. **Supervised Clinical Re-Evaluation:** Require the student to re-administer the assessment (e.g., SPM-2 or structured clinical observations) under direct faculty observation in the pediatric lab.
4. **Live Clinical Reasoning Defense:** Have the student verbally defend their intervention choices, safety contraindications, and SMART goals before resubmitting the written documentation.
