### 1.1 The High Stakes of Pre-Flight Briefings in FAA Part 141 Flight Training

Under Federal Aviation Regulation **14 CFR § 91.103 (Preflight Action)**, each pilot in command must, before beginning a flight, become familiar with all available information concerning that flight, including:

1. **Weather Reports and Forecasts:** Decoding live METARs, Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts (TAFs), Pilot Weather Reports (PIREPs), Graphical Forecasts for Aviation (GFAs), and convective SIGMETs/AIRMETs.
2. **Fuel Requirements:** Calculating precise fuel burn rates, climb reserves, cruise endurance, and statutory 30-minute daytime (or 45-minute nighttime) VFR reserves under 14 CFR § 91.151 (and 45-minute IFR reserves under § 91.167).
3. **Runway Lengths & Takeoff/Landing Distances:** Accurately interpolating Pilot’s Operating Handbook (POH) performance charts against pressure altitude, ambient temperature, headwind/crosswind components, and runway surface conditions.
4. **Air Traffic Delays and NOTAMs:** Identifying temporary flight restrictions (TFRs), unserviceable navigational aids (VORs/NDBs), closed runways, and active military operating areas (MOAs).

In a Part 141 curriculum, ground instructors, Assistant Chiefs, and Check Airmen require students to submit written pre-flight dossiers detailing their operational reasoning. These writeups assess whether a student pilot possesses the cognitive framework required to recognize impending hazards, calculate critical abort points, and execute sound **Aeronautical Decision-Making (ADM)**.

Generative LLMs produce remarkably articulate, professionally structured prose filled with standard aviation jargon. However, underneath this veneer of fluency lies catastrophic operational misinformation.

#### 2. Density Altitude & Weight-and-Balance Fabrications
Density altitude calculations require precise mathematical conversions: calculating pressure altitude from local altimeter settings, then applying the non-standard temperature deviation against ISA (International Standard Atmosphere: 15°C - 2°C per 1,000 ft). LLMs regularly hallucinate intermediate mathematical steps or invent baseline sea-level performance figures for high-altitude mountain airports (e.g., Leadville KLXV or Flagstaff KFLG). Furthermore, LLMs frequently fabricate Center of Gravity (CG) moment arms, placing an overloaded training aircraft safely within the envelope when manual calculation shows an aft-CG stall-spin trap.

#### 3. Fabricated NOTAMs & Closed Runway Omissions
NOTAM processing requires meticulous parsing of domestic FAA and ICAO alphanumeric codes. Generative models commonly invent synthetic NOTAMs (e.g., *"Runway 10/28 closed for mowing"*) while completely missing critical real-world notices such as unlighted 400-foot construction cranes on final approach, GPS RAIM outages along RNAV routes, or temporary tower closures.

#### 4. Out-of-Sequence & Non-POH Emergency Checklists
Perhaps the most dangerous failure mode occurs when LLMs generate emergency procedure flows. Aircraft manufacturers (e.g., Cessna, Piper, Cirrus, Diamond) establish strict, FAA-certified emergency memory items in the Pilot’s Operating Handbook / Airplane Flight Manual (POH/AFM). 

In an in-flight engine fire scenario, the POH-mandated flow for a typical single-engine aircraft requires immediate fuel cutoff, mixture idle cut-off, fuel pump off, and cabin heat/air closed to prevent toxic flame entry. LLMs frequently scramble these critical sequences—suggesting auxiliary fuel pumps be turned *on* (which pumps high-pressure avgas directly onto an engine fire) or advising unvetted glide configurations that sacrifice precious altitude before establishing best glide speed (Vbg).

In an aeronautical science writeup, authentic writing is *inherently* low in perplexity and low in burstiness. The **FAA Airman Certification Standards (ACS)**, Federal Aviation Regulations, and aircraft POH manuals demand strict adherence to standardized terminology. Maintain Vbg (68 KIAS), select landing site within 30 degrees of centerline, fuel shutoff valve OFF, mixture IDLE CUTOFF, ignition OFF, flaps as required, master switch OFF prior to touchdown."*

Because this passage contains memorized POH boldface items, standardized speeds (Vx, Vy, Vbg), and rigid procedural steps, generic AI detectors routinely classify it as **85%–95% AI-generated**.

### 4.1 Weather Briefings & Telemetry Concordance (METAR, TAF, PIREP, NOTAM, Radar/Convective Analysis)

In an authentic weather briefing writeup, a student pilot accesses the FAA Aviation Weather Center or an approved EFB, retrieves active raw alphanumeric reports, and translates them into operational flight impacts:

* **METAR Interpretation:** Extracting ceiling, visibility, temperature/dewpoint spread (carburetor icing potential), altimeter setting, and peak gusts.
* **TAF Evolution:** Projecting how visual meteorological conditions (VMC) may deteriorate into marginal VFR (MVFR) or instrument meteorological conditions (IMC) along the flight corridor over a 4-hour window.
* **Winds and Temperatures Aloft (FB):** Selecting optimal cruising altitudes based on headwind components, freezing levels, and true airspeed performance.
* **NOTAM Evaluation:** Checking runway lighting outages, taxiway closures, obstructions, and airspace restrictions along the route.

**The Generative AI Infiltration:**
When prompted, LLMs frequently synthesize plausible but completely fabricated METAR/TAF codes. An LLM may generate a weather brief based on typical historical averages rather than live conditions, completely omitting an active squall line or low-level wind shear alert (LLWS). Faculty can immediately spot this by cross-referencing the writeup's cited weather data against archival NOAA/NWS data for the assigned flight planning date.

### 4.3 Aeronautical Decision-Making (ADM) Frameworks

The FAA requires pilots to master structured ADM models to eliminate hazardous attitudes (Anti-Authority, Impulsivity, Invulnerability, Macho, Resignation):

* **The DECIDE Model:**
  * **D**etect the fact that a change has occurred.
  * **E**stimate the need to counter or react to the change.
  * **C**hoose a desirable outcome for the success of the flight.
  * **I**dentify actions which could successfully control the change.
  * **D**o the necessary action to adapt to the change.
  * **E**valuate the effect of the action.
* **The 5P Model:** Plan, Plane, Pilot, Passengers, Programming.
* **The PAVE Checklist:** Pilot (IMSAFE), Aircraft (airworthiness, equipment), enVironment (weather, terrain), External Pressures.
* **The IMSAFE Checklist:** Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion.
* **The 3P Model:** Perceive, Process, Perform.

**The Generative AI Infiltration:**
AI-generated ADM writeups are characterized by high-level generalizations and formulaic platitudes. The writeup will state *"I will use the DECIDE model to evaluate any changes in the flight,"* but completely fails to identify concrete trigger points (e.g., *"If cloud bases drop below 2,500 ft AGL at Checkpoint Delta, I will execute an immediate 180-degree standard-rate turn toward my alternate, KBAK, which has an active ceiling of 4,500 ft and 10 miles visibility"*).

### 4.4 In-Flight Emergency Diversion Trees & System Failures

In flight writeups, students are tasked with establishing emergency diversion decision trees:

1. **Immediate Glide Configuration:** Trimming for Vbg, determining best glide range radius (1.5 NM per 1,000 ft AGL in standard trainers).
2. **Forced Landing Site Selection:** Evaluating wind direction, field length, terrain slope, surface obstacles (power lines, ditches), and proximity to civilization.
3. **Troubleshooting & Cause Identification:** Systematic fuel selector switching, auxiliary fuel pump activation, mixture adjustment, magneto checking, primer locked.
4. **Emergency Communication & Transponder Codes:** Setting transponder to Squawk 7700, broadcasting Mayday on 121.5 MHz or active ATC frequency, activating 406 MHz ELT.

**The Generative AI Infiltration:**
LLMs regularly produce generic emergency checklists that blend procedures across different aircraft types—such as recommending retractable gear extension procedures or constant-speed propeller pitch settings for a fixed-gear, fixed-pitch Cessna 172—or recommending procedures that violate specific POH emergency directives.
