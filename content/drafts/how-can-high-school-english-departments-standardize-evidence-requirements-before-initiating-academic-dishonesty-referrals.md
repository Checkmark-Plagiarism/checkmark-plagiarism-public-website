### 1.1 The Inequity of "Hunch-Based" Enforcement Across Faculty

The most pervasive systemic failure in secondary writing programs is **inter-instructor variance**. When high school English departments leave the evidentiary threshold for academic dishonesty to individual teacher discretion, students experience wildly disparate treatment for identical drafting behaviors.

#### 1. Multilingual and English Language Learner (ELL) False Positives
Peer-reviewed linguistic research (including landmark studies from Stanford University) has repeatedly established that commercial, whole-document probabilistic AI detectors exhibit severe systemic bias against non-native English writers. 

Because multilingual learners frequently employ standard sentence constructions, lower lexical perplexity (predictable word choices), and constrained transitional phrases, generic statistical detectors routinely misclassify their authentic prose as machine-generated. When a teacher initiates an administrative referral based solely on a monolithic percentage score, multilingual students are disproportionately targeted for disciplinary action.

#### 2. Neurodivergent Writing Telemetry Misinterpretation
Students with Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), or specific learning differences frequently demonstrate non-traditional writing behaviors. 

A student experiencing hyperfocus may type 800 words in an uninterrupted 25-minute sprint with minimal backspacing; another student may draft disconnected argumentative bullet points in an external notes app before pasting them into the document to organize them. When evaluated through subjective teacher intuition or uncalibrated paste detectors, these neurodivergent drafting patterns are erroneously flagged as "illicit copying" or "outsourced text."

#### 3. Stylistic "Voice" Intuition and Implicit Deficit Tracking
Perhaps the most dangerous evidentiary standard is the subjective teacher claim: *"This doesn't sound like their voice."* 

When teachers rely on gut impressions of student capability, systemic biases emerge. Historically marginalized students, students from under-resourced middle schools, or students with prior behavioral records who achieve a sudden breakthrough in analytical sophistication are met with suspicion rather than celebration. Without concrete behavioral and textual receipts, "voice hunches" invite unconscious bias into high-stakes disciplinary determinations.

#### 1. *Goss v. Lopez* (1975) and Procedural Due Process
In *Goss v. Lopez* (419 U.S. 565), the United States Supreme Court affirmed that public school students have a constitutionally protected property interest in their education and a liberty interest in their academic standing and reputation. 

Before an institution can impose disciplinary sanctions—including suspensions, notations on permanent transcripts, revocation of academic honors, or removal from Advanced Placement (AP)/International Baccalaureate (IB) courses—the school must satisfy foundational procedural due process requirements:
- Timely oral or written notice of the specific factual allegations.
- Clear disclosure of the objective basis and evidence supporting the accusation.
- A fair and impartial opportunity for the student to present their defense and contextualize their work.

When an English teacher refers a student for disciplinary sanction based on an opaque, proprietary AI probability score, the school cannot articulate the factual basis of the accusation. A statistical probability is an assertion of likelihood, not an eyewitness or forensic demonstration of misconduct.

#### 2. FERPA 34 CFR Part 99 § 99.10 Record Inspection Rights
Under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), parents and eligible students have the explicit legal right to inspect and review all education records directly related to the student maintained by the institution (34 CFR § 99.10). 

If a teacher submits a disciplinary referral form citing an AI detector output, the underlying algorithm's calculations, training weights, and decision rules are proprietary trade secrets of third-party vendors. The school district cannot produce the source code, training corpus, or algorithmic rationale for parent inspection. 

When parents retain legal counsel or file formal administrative appeals, school districts are forced to overturn disciplinary actions and expunge student records because the evidence cannot withstand statutory inspection.

#### 3. The Evidentiary Collapse in Administrative Grievance Hearings
When academic dishonesty referrals reach the Assistant Principal, Principal, Academic Dean, or District Hearing Officer, the burden of proof rests upon the institution (typically under the *preponderance of the evidence* standard, requiring greater than 50% certainty based on credible, verifiable facts). 

Probabilistic AI scores fail this standard because detector vendors themselves include disclaimers acknowledging that false positives occur. In an appeal hearing, an integrity officer cannot prove whether an 80% AI score represents an 80% probability of whole-paper generation, 80% AI-generated text, or a 100% authentic paper matching statistical patterns in the detector's training data. Without physical telemetry or side-by-side textual proof, the case collapses.

### 2.1 Tier 1: Disallowed Evidence (Speculative & Probabilistic)

Tier 1 consists of indicators that are **strictly inadmissible** as the sole or primary basis for an academic dishonesty referral or grade penalty. 

While these markers may prompt an educator to inspect deeper writing telemetry, they carry zero adjudicative weight on their own:

1. **Monolithic Whole-Paper AI Percentages:** Any single score claiming a document is "X% AI" generated. These scores represent uncalibrated statistical heuristics that cannot identify which specific sentences were generated or distinguish authentic writing from assisted brainstorming.
2. **Subjective Stylistic and "Voice" Intuition:** Subjective claims such as *"This doesn't sound like something a 10th grader would write"* or *"The vocabulary is too elevated for this student."*
3. **Unverified Gross Similarity Percentages:** Raw aggregate similarity index scores (e.g., "28% Similarity") that include properly formatted direct quotations, works cited entries, standard assignment prompts, or common domain-specific phrases.
4. **Third-Party Readability or "Humanizer" Metric Flags:** Flesch-Kincaid grade level anomalies or third-party "AI-humanizer" scores that lack physical, source-verified textual matching.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Why must high school English departments ban whole-paper AI percentage scores as disciplinary evidence?
Whole-paper AI detector percentages represent probabilistic heuristics calculated from statistical language models. They do not provide physical, verifiable proof of authorship and suffer from documented false-positive rates—particularly among multilingual learners, neurodivergent students, and advanced writers with sophisticated syntax. Relying on an uncorroborated percentage violates *Goss v. Lopez* due process protections and exposes districts to successful administrative and legal appeals.

### 2. How does Checkmark’s Essay Playback™ protect students who are falsely accused of AI writing?
Essay Playback™ captures the complete temporal history of the writing session stroke-by-stroke. When an honest student is falsely flagged by a legacy detector, Essay Playback™ provides indisputable evidence of authentic human authorship: showing hours of drafting time, organic typing burst velocities, natural cognitive pauses, sentence restructuring, and iterative backspacing.

### 3. What should an English teacher do if a student pastes text from an external source?
Under Checkmark’s Tier 2 protocol, an external paste event is treated as an inquiry prompt rather than an automatic violation. The teacher conducts a 5-minute pre-flight conference to review the student's research notes. Checkmark’s paste buffer forensics preserves the exact text that was pasted, allowing the teacher to easily verify whether the student pasted legitimate research notes, direct quotations, or unauthorized AI-generated drafts.

### 4. How does the 3-Tier Evidentiary Standard reduce teacher grading fatigue and burnout?
By establishing clear, objective criteria, teachers no longer spend hours hunting down obscure URLs, agonizing over ambiguous stylistic hunches, or bracing for hostile parent confrontations. ### 5. What is the role of the English Department Chair in the referral workflow?
