### 2.2 Roman Numeral Analysis & Figured Bass Confabulations

Roman Numeral Analysis (RNA) requires identifying the root, quality, inversion, and functional harmonic purpose of every vertical sonority within a tonal context. Generative LLMs frequently make foundational errors in standard AP convention:

#### 1. Cadential 6/4 Misclassification:
* **The Error:** LLMs regularly label a cadential six-four chord as a standalone tonic inversion: `I6/4 - V7 - I`.
* **AP Music Theory Standard:** In the AP curriculum and common-practice theory, the cadential six-four is functionally a dominant harmony with double suspensions/appoggiaturas resolving down by step (6–5 and 4–3 above the bass). It must be analyzed as `V6/4-5/3` or `Cad 6/4 - V - I`. An AI writeup asserting that "the tonic triad appears in second inversion to provide stable harmonic resting" reveals a total misunderstanding of dominant prolongation.

#### 2. Seventh Chord Inversion Scrambling:
* **The Error:** Inverting figured bass numbers or failing to distinguish between `V7` (root position), `V6/5` (first inversion, 3rd in bass), `V4/3` (second inversion, 5th in bass), and `V4/2` (third inversion, 7th in bass).
* **Classroom Example:** An AI will frequently describe a bass note moving from `Fa` to `Mi` as a "root-position dominant resolving to tonic," missing that `Fa` in the bass necessitates a `V4/2` resolving to a first-inversion tonic `I6`.

#### 3. Secondary Dominant & Leading-Tone Confusions:
* **The Error:** Mislabeling secondary dominants (e.g., `V/V`, `V7/iv`, `vii°7/V`) as random modal borrowings or modulating to foreign keys for a single passing chord.
* **Classroom Example:** In C Major, encountering a `D - F# - A - C` chord resolving to `G - B - D`. An LLM often writes: *"The piece briefly modulates to G major and establishes a new tonic,"* rather than recognizing the local secondary dominant function (`V7/V`) that remains anchored in the home key.

#### 4. Chromatic Predominant Harmonies (Neapolitan & Augmented Sixths):
* **The Error:** Misidentifying the **Neapolitan Sixth (`N6` or `bII6`)** as a standard minor second chord, or confusing the three distinct national varieties of **Augmented Sixth chords**:
  - **Italian 6th (`It+6`):** `Le - Do - Fi` (1 tonic, 1 3rd, augmented 6th).
  - **French 6th (`Fr+6`):** `Le - Do - Re - Fi` (includes scale degree 2, containing a whole-tone feel).
  - **German 6th (`Gr+6`):** `Le - Do - Me - Fi` (includes minor 3rd, resolves to `V6/4-5/3` to avoid parallel 5ths).
* Generative bots routinely invent fifths in Italian sixth chords or describe French sixth chords as diminished triads.

#### Major Voice-Leading Rule Deductions Overlooked by AI:
1. **Parallel Fifths and Octaves:** Two voices moving in the same direction by parallel motion from one perfect fifth/octave to another. LLMs cannot calculate vertical intervals between moving horizontal vectors simultaneously.
2. **Direct (Hidden) Fifths and Octaves:** Soprano and bass moving in similar motion into a perfect fifth or octave with a leap in the soprano voice.
3. **Unresolved Leading Tones:** In outer voices, scale degree `7` (leading tone) must resolve up by half step to `1` (tonic) in dominant-to-tonic progressions (`V - I` or `V7 - I`). AI writeups routinely leave leading tones unresolved or drop them down by third.
4. **Seventh Resolutions:** The chordal 7th of any seventh chord (`V7`, `iiø7`, `vii°7`) must resolve downward by step. AI models routinely leap upward from the seventh to double a root.
5. **Improper Doubling & Voice Crossing:** In root-position major/minor triads, doubling the root is standard. Doubling the leading tone or doubling the 3rd in a major triad (except in deceptive `V - vi` progressions) incurs major AP scoring penalties. Voice crossing (e.g., tenor singing above alto) or voice overlap severely disrupts SATB clarity.

### 2.4 Score-to-Prose Concordance Mismatch

When generative AI tools are prompted with incomplete musical information or low-resolution score images, they confabulate structural features that contradict the actual musical notation:

* **Modulation Hallucinations:** Generative bots frequently declare transitions to distant foreign keys (such as modulating from E-flat Major to F-sharp Minor) when the music simply contains an unaccented chromatic passing tone.
* **Clef Misreadings:** LLMs regularly misinterpret alto clef (viola) or tenor clef (cello/bassoon) as treble clef, shifting every note in their harmonic narrative by a third or a fifth.
* **Cadence Misclassification:** Confusing Deceptive Cadences (`V - vi` or `V - VI`) with Authentic Cadences (`V - I`), or failing to notice that an imperfect authentic cadence (IAC) has scale degree 3 or 5 in the soprano rather than the tonic scale degree 1.
