### 1.1 The Technical Anatomy of Modern Text "Humanization"

To establish fair evidentiary standards, hearing boards must understand the exact computer science mechanisms behind automated text rewriting and "humanizing" software:

* **Synonym Permutation & Thesaurus Swapping:** Tools like QuillBot and legacy word spinners replace high-frequency academic vocabulary with less common synonyms. While this alters surface n-grams, it often introduces semantic incongruities, archaic phrasing, or broken idioms.
* **Perplexity and Burstiness Manipulation:** Specialized evasion tools (such as Undetectable AI, BypassGPT, and StealthWriter) analyze the mathematical predictability (perplexity) and sentence-length variation (burstiness) of the input text. They deliberately insert unusual transition words, fragmented sentences, and erratic punctuation to trick static neural classifiers into classifying the text as human-authored.
* **Intentional Dysfluency Injection:** Advanced humanizers intentionally introduce mild stylistic awkwardness, non-standard phrasing, and idiosyncratic syntax designed to emulate human error without triggering basic spell-check flags.
* **Iterative Multi-Prompt Re-generation:** Students increasingly utilize meta-prompts within frontier models (e.g., *"Rewrite this essay using an informal tone, varied sentence lengths, occasional colloquialisms, and a Flesch-Kincaid grade level of 10"*), bypassing simple heuristics.

### 2.1 The Constitutional Due Process Framework

Under landmark legal precedents, public college and university students hold constitutionally protected property and liberty interests in their continued education, academic standing, and professional reputation:

* ***Goss v. Lopez*, 419 U.S. 565 (1975):** Established that disciplinary suspensions require fundamental procedural safeguards: timely written notice of specific charges, disclosure of the evidence, and an opportunity for the student to present their version of events before an impartial arbiter.
* ***Dixon v. Alabama State Board of Education*, 294 F.2d 150 (5th Cir. 1961):** Held that due process in higher education disciplinary proceedings requires an explanation of the evidence supporting the charge and a fair hearing before sanctions are imposed.
* ***Board of Curators of the University of Missouri v. Horowitz*, 435 U.S. 78 (1978):** While granting greater deference to purely academic evaluations, the Supreme Court emphasized that disciplinary sanctions or dismissals carrying moral opprobrium (such as fraud or academic dishonesty) demand procedural protections.
* **The "Arbitrary and Capricious" Doctrine:** Courts will overturn university disciplinary decisions if the institution acts arbitrarily, violates its own published procedures, or renders a finding unsupported by substantial evidence in the administrative record.

---

### 2.2 Standards of Proof Compared: Preponderance vs. Clear and Convincing

Institutions universally specify a standard of proof in their Academic Integrity Bylaws or Student Codes of Conduct. Committees must precisely apply the standard mandated for the specific sanction sought:

#### 1. Preponderance of the Evidence (The "More Likely Than Not" Standard: >50% Probability)
* **Application:** Standard across the vast majority of university honor councils and K-12 school boards for routine classroom violations, course-level grade reductions (e.g., receiving a zero on an assignment or an 'F' in the course), and initial honor warnings.
* **Evidentiary Threshold:** The evidence presented must demonstrate that the allegation of unauthorized AI rewriting or humanizer use is more likely true than not true.
* **Application to AI Claims:** A probabilistic AI score of 75% from a detector does **not** equal a 75% legal probability of misconduct. Because neural classifiers operate with systemic error rates, false positives on ELL text, and vulnerability to humanizers, a raw percentage fails to satisfy the preponderance standard unless corroborated by independent, empirical process telemetry.

#### 2. Clear and Convincing Evidence (The "Substantially More Likely" Standard: ~75–80% Certainty)
* **Application:** Required by many elite universities, law schools, medical schools, graduate programs, and institutional codes whenever severe disciplinary sanctions are on the table—such as permanent transcript notations (e.g., 'XF' grade), academic suspension, expulsion, or degree revocation.
* **Evidentiary Threshold:** The evidence must produce in the minds of the committee members a firm belief or conviction that the factual claims are substantially true. The proof must be clear, explicit, and unclouded by substantial doubt.
* **Application to AI Claims:** It is legally impossible for a black-box AI detector percentage alone to satisfy the Clear and Convincing standard. To sustain a suspension or expulsion for AI rewriting, the hearing file **must contain incontrovertible digital forensic artifacts** (such as keystroke telemetry, external paste logs, and immutable clipboard preservation).

## 4. Deconstructing the 4 Most Common Student Defenses in Humanizer & LLM Rewrite Cases

When confronted with academic dishonesty allegations involving AI rewriting, students rarely confess immediately. Instead, they present sophisticated technical and procedural defenses. Academic integrity panels must be equipped to audit each defense using empirical writing telemetry.

### Defense 1: "I only used QuillBot or Grammarly to fix basic grammar and spelling!"

* **The Student's Argument:** The student acknowledges using an automated tool but claims authorized, benign assistance (e.g., fixing commas, subject-verb agreement, or spelling errors) permitted under general course policies.
* **The Underlying Reality:** The student drafted an initial outline or raw AI generation, fed full paragraphs into QuillBot's "Paraphrase / Fluency / Creative" mode, and replaced entire sentence structures with algorithmic rewrites, thereby transferring core syntactic authorship to the machine.
* **The Committee Telemetry Audit Protocol:**
  1. Open Checkmark's **External Paste Buffer Inspector**.
  2. Inspect the timestamped clipboard insertion logs. If the paste log shows an insertion of a 300-word block from an external window within a 2-second interval, examine the preserved text.
  3. Compare the preserved pasted text against the student's final prose. If the student pasted pre-paraphrased text directly into the canvas rather than using inline spelling suggestions, the claim of "routine grammar checking" is empirically disproven.
  4. Launch **Essay Playback™**. In authentic grammar tool usage (e.g., Grammarly browser extension), the playback reveals active composing pauses, individual word-level replacements, and manual acceptance of suggestions. In contrast, bulk paraphrase evasion displays large external block pastes followed by zero sentence-level composing effort.

---

### Defense 2: "I wrote the entire paper offline in Apple Notes / MS Word and pasted it in at the end!"

* **The Student's Argument:** The student explains the presence of massive external paste blocks by asserting that they composed the essay in an external, unmonitored desktop application or mobile app and transferred the finished draft into Google Docs or the Canvas LMS editor immediately prior to submission.
* **The Underlying Reality:** The student generated the essay in ChatGPT or ran an AI draft through Undetectable AI / BypassGPT in an external browser tab and pasted the evasion output directly into the submission box.
* **The Committee Telemetry Audit Protocol:**
  1. **Examine the File Metadata and Local Revision Logs:** Request the native `.docx` file or Apple Notes export. In authentic Microsoft Word offline drafting, the file's internal XML structure (`app.xml` and `core.xml`) records total editing time, revision numbers, and creation timestamps. A 2,500-word Word document with a "Total Editing Time" of 3 minutes is conclusive proof of external copying.
  2. **Audit the Drafting Progression in Canvas / Google Docs:** Review the document history. If the document was created at 11:42 PM and the entire 2,000-word text was pasted at 11:43 PM with zero prior character keystrokes, the student bears the burden of producing contemporaneous offline drafts, outlines, or version histories.
  3. **Apply the Presumption Rule:** Establish an institutional rule: students who draft outside the prescribed LMS/Docs environment without contemporaneous electronic evidence forfeit the benefit of process telemetry.

---

### Defense 3: "The AI detector is biased against my non-native English writing style (ESL/ELL)!"

* **The Student's Argument:** An international or multilingual student asserts that their formal, structured, and occasionally repetitive prose was falsely flagged by a static AI detector due to known algorithmic bias against non-native writers.
* **The Underlying Reality:** This is often a completely legitimate and factually accurate defense. Numerous peer-reviewed studies confirm that traditional AI detectors misclassify ESL writing as machine-generated at rates exceeding 30% due to uniform lexical density and predictable syntax.
* **The Committee Telemetry Audit Protocol:**
  1. **Immediately Dismiss Standalone Percentage Scores:** The committee must formally set aside the generic AI score to ensure non-discriminatory adjudication.
  2. **Analyze the Temporal Telemetry in Essay Playback™:** Review the student's actual drafting session. Authentic ELL drafting displays distinctive, undeniable cognitive human patterns:
     * Frequent dictionary/translation pauses (bursts of typing followed by 45–90 second pauses while the student consults vocabulary resources);
     * High backspace and cursor revision rates in prepositional phrases and verb tenses;
     * Organic paragraph expansion where sentences are reworked over multiple editing passes.
  3. **Outcome:** If Essay Playback™ confirms natural, effortful composition over several hours, the student is **completely and definitively exonerated**, protecting them from a devastating false accusation.

---

### Defense 4: "I took handwritten notes and retyped my research into the document quickly!"

* **The Student's Argument:** The student explains steady, rapid typing with minimal pauses by claiming they were merely transcribing their own handwritten research notes or an offline paper outline.
* **The Underlying Reality:** The student placed a smartphone or second monitor next to their laptop displaying a ChatGPT/Undetectable AI output window and manually retyped the text character-by-character to evade simple paste-detection scripts (**Transcription Evasion**).
* **The Committee Telemetry Audit Protocol:**
  1. **Examine Typing Velocity & Burst Uniformity:** In genuine human composing, typing velocity fluctuates dramatically (e.g., 65 WPM during an idea burst, dropping to 10 WPM or 0 WPM during cognitive formulation). In transcription evasion, typing velocity exhibits mechanical, steady uniformity (e.g., a flat 45–55 WPM across 1,500 words with zero conceptual pauses).
  2. **Audit Error Patterns and Backspace Telemetry:** Authentic human writing contains cognitive mid-sentence revisions (e.g., starting a clause, backspacing 6 words, restructuring the thought). Retyping from a screen exhibits purely mechanical typographical errors (e.g., hitting adjacent keys, skipping a line of text, or retyping OCR artifacts) without conceptual restructuring.
  3. **Conduct a Targeted Viva Voce:** Ask the student to explain the complex vocabulary, syntax, and source syntheses present in the retyped passages. A student who genuinely synthesized handwritten research notes will easily explain their conceptual logic; a student who mechanically transcribed an LLM output will struggle to explain the prose.

### Phase 1: Pre-Hearing Intake & Evidentiary Triage (The Gatekeeper Rule)

Before an academic integrity referral is scheduled for a formal hearing, the Academic Integrity Officer or Honor Council Chair must perform an evidentiary triage:

1. **The "No Black-Box Referral" Rule:** If an instructor submits a referral supported solely by a whole-document AI detector percentage (e.g., *"GPTZero says 85% AI"* or *"Turnitin AI report attached"*), the referral is **rejected at intake**. The reporting instructor is instructed to compile corroborating process evidence or telemetry before the case can proceed.
2. **Jurisdictional & Policy Scope Check:** Verify whether the instructor's syllabus explicitly prohibited AI rewriting tools, paraphrasers, or automated spinners. If the course policy was ambiguous (e.g., stating only *"AI tools may be used ethically for editing"* without defining limits), the committee must consider whether the student had adequate notice under due process principles.
3. **Threshold Prima Facie Determination:** Ensure the case file contains sufficient prima facie evidence (e.g., suspicious paste logs, absence of drafting history, or unverified source citations) to warrant a formal proceeding.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Can a university legally be sued for suspending a student based solely on a commercial AI detector score?
**Yes.** Higher education institutions and public school districts have faced serious legal challenges, formal board grievances, and civil rights complaints when imposing severe academic sanctions based exclusively on black-box AI detection scores. Because commercial detectors operate with unverified proprietary algorithms, have demonstrated error rates, and lack verifiable audit trails, relying on them as sole evidence violates procedural due process (*Goss v. Lopez*) and fails the administrative law standard of substantial evidence. Institutions must substantiate allegations with empirical process telemetry, such as Essay Playback™ and external paste logs.

### Q2: How does Checkmark differentiate between authorized grammar checking (e.g., Grammarly) and unauthorized text humanizing (e.g., Undetectable AI)?
Checkmark differentiates through **temporal interaction dynamics and paste preservation**. Authorized grammar tools typically suggest word-level or phrase-level adjustments accepted sequentially by the student, showing continuous human engagement, pause patterns, and manual keystroke acceptance in Essay Playback™. In contrast, text humanizers (such as Undetectable AI, BypassGPT, or StealthWriter) require the student to export entire drafts to an external website, process them through a neural rewriter, and paste large blocks of modified text back into the editor in a single timestamped event. Checkmark's External Paste Buffer captures the full inserted block and preserves the exact text, allowing committees to review the precise imported content.

### Q3: What should an Academic Integrity Committee do if an instructor brings an AI case from a static PDF submission with no revision history?
If an essay was submitted as a static PDF or plain Word document without revision history or telemetry tracking, the committee must treat the case with extreme caution. The committee should:
1. Reject any standalone whole-paper AI percentage score as insufficient to meet the Preponderance of the Evidence standard.
2. Conduct a structured **Viva Voce (Oral Defense)**, asking the student to explain their analytical arguments, thesis evolution, and source selections.
3. Request contemporaneous drafting artifacts from the student (e.g., version histories, research outlines, browser search histories, or library book records).
4. If no verifiable evidence exists from either side, the committee must **dismiss the charge without prejudice**, as the institution has failed to satisfy its initial burden of proof.

### Q4: How does Checkmark protect English Language Learners (ELL) from false AI allegations during committee reviews?
Generic AI detectors frequently trigger false positives on ELL writing because non-native writers often utilize simpler sentence structures and predictable vocabulary. Checkmark protects ELL students by shifting the focus entirely away from fallible whole-text statistical guesses to **objective temporal drafting evidence**. Through Essay Playback™, an ELL student can clearly show their authentic human drafting journey—complete with natural composing pauses, translation breaks, vocabulary lookups, and organic sentence revisions. This keystroke-level proof provides definitive, objective exoneration.

### Q5: Does reviewing keystroke telemetry and Essay Playback™ violate student privacy under FERPA?
**No.** Keystroke telemetry, revision logs, and session replays captured within institutionally managed educational software (such as Canvas, Buzz LMS, or Google Workspace for Education) constitute legitimate **Educational Records** under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA, 34 CFR Part 99). These records are securely maintained with enterprise-grade encryption and access controls. Furthermore, under FERPA § 99.10, students and parents have the affirmative statutory right to inspect and review these exact digital records during an academic disciplinary proceeding.

### Q6: Can a student fake authentic typing dynamics by using automated typing bots or macros?
While theoretically possible, automated typing scripts (such as browser extensions designed to simulate typing) exhibit distinct, non-human mathematical signatures. Macro typing runs at perfectly uniform millisecond intervals (e.g., exactly 28ms per character) across hundreds of words, with zero natural cognitive pauses between clauses and zero authentic typographical corrections. Checkmark's typing velocity algorithms instantly identify this unnatural, flat rhythm as mechanical transcription or macro insertion, flagging it for hearing panel review.

### Q7: If a student rewrites an external paste word-for-word after inserting it, does Checkmark still catch it?
**Yes.** Checkmark's External Paste Buffer records and permanently preserves the full original text of every external clipboard insertion the instant it enters the document. Even if the student subsequently spends hours manually editing, replacing synonyms, or restructuring sentences in an attempt to hide the paste, the committee can access the timestamped paste log, view the original raw text that was imported, and use the "Jump to Playback" feature to watch the entire manual obfuscation process unfold.

### Q8: How should an Academic Integrity Committee handle retroactive appeals of AI misconduct penalties from previous semesters?
When a student files a retroactive appeal alleging they were improperly penalized based solely on an uncalibrated AI detector score, the appeals board should review the original case file for procedural due process compliance. If the original sanction was based exclusively on a single percentage score without supporting process telemetry, paste logs, or source matching, the institution should grant the appeal, expunge the disciplinary notation, and establish a policy requiring telemetry-backed evidence for all future proceedings.
