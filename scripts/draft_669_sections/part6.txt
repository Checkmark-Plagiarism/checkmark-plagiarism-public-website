## 8. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1: Is it acceptable for student reporters to use AI to improve the grammar of a peer's quote?
**No.** In professional and scholastic journalism (NSPA/JEA standards), quote marks indicate the exact spoken words of the source. While journalists may remove minor verbal filler ('um,' 'like') if it does not alter the meaning, running quotes through generative AI transforms them into synthetic corporate rewrites and constitutes unethical manipulation. If a quote is grammatically confusing or curt, the reporter should conduct a brief follow-up interview or paraphrase the information outside quotation marks.

### Q2: What should an adviser do if a senior parent submits an AI-generated quote for a senior tribute ad?
Parent-purchased tribute advertisements represent personal messages rather than student journalistic reporting. However, advisers should maintain clear publication policies regarding offensive, copyrighted, or synthetic content. If a parent tribute contains obvious LLM hallucinations, advisers should contact the family directly to confirm their intended message before printing.

### Q3: Why did a generic AI detector flag our student's AP-style sports lead as 95% AI?
Associated Press (AP) style requires concise, standardized, formulaic writing (who, what, where, when, why) with specific date and title formatting. Because formulaic journalistic syntax has naturally low linguistic perplexity (high statistical predictability), generic statistical detectors misinterpret high-quality journalistic training as machine-generated text. Checkmark Essay Playback resolves this by showing the student's authentic drafting process.

### Q4: How does Essay Playback distinguish between typing from thought versus typing off an AI screen?
When drafting from thought, human writers exhibit variable typing speeds, frequent backspaces, sentence-level pauses, and organic word replacements. When typing while listening to audio, writers display periodic listening pauses (3-6 seconds) after short word bursts. When manually transcribing from an AI screen (such as a phone), typing speeds are unusually steady, mechanical, and continuous, lacking composing pauses or audio replay cycles.

### Q5: How can advisers prevent students from creating fake quotes when sources ignore interview requests?
Advisers should implement a proactive **'No Ghosting' Newsroom Protocol**: establish mandatory 48-hour interview confirmation windows, teach students how to approach peers in person during homeroom or advisory periods, and create a designated backup source list for every feature spread before layout begins.

### Q6: Can students use AI transcription tools like Otter.ai to transcribe recorded interviews?
**Yes.** Using AI transcription software to convert authentic spoken audio into text is an industry-standard journalistic practice that enhances accuracy. To maintain integrity, students should upload both the raw audio file and the transcript to the staff repository, noting the exact audio timestamp for every pulled quote.

### Q7: Does Checkmark store student yearbook copy to train future AI models?
**No.** Checkmark maintains strict zero-retention data privacy standards. Student submissions are never used to train or fine-tune AI models, ensuring complete FERPA and COPPA compliance for high school districts.
